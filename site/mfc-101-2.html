One-High and Two-High Shells

A shell is the safeties’ starting structure before the snap. Use it to choose where to look first—not to declare the coverage before the ball moves.

Decision skill: use deep-safety structure as a clue, then verify rotation.
In this course: Compare shellsFirst checksConfirm itMadden labCheck
Learning objective

Use deep-safety structure to choose your first post-snap check.

By the snap, say: “I see a two-high shell. I will count the box, then watch whether either safety rotates when the ball moves.”

A shell is a starting structure. The post-snap movement is the evidence that tells you whether your original plan still works.

The visual distinction

Count deep safeties first.

Two-high shell

Two safeties begin deep, splitting the field. This picture often creates room underneath or changes the run count. It can still rotate after the snap.

One-high shell

One safety begins deep in the middle. This picture often leaves an extra defender closer to the box or creates different outside leverage. It can also rotate.

The line of scrimmage, offensive formation, and front stay comparable. The teaching variable is deep-safety placement—not a vague “defense blob.”

What the shell changes

Let the shell choose your first check.

TWO-HIGH

Start with the box and underneath space.

With two deep safeties visible, count the near-line defenders. If the numbers are favorable, the run may be your first check. If corners play off, quick throws can be your safe answer.

ONE-HIGH

Start with middle-field and outside leverage.

With one deep middle safety, identify where the extra near-line defender is and how the corners align. This helps you choose a concept to inspect after the snap.

Rule: “One-high” and “two-high” describe where safeties are before the snap. They do not, by themselves, prove a specific coverage.
The confirmation step

Watch the safeties move when the ball moves.

After the snap, the safety rotation is evidence. A two-high picture can rotate to one-high. A one-high picture can rotate down or disguise help. Your job is to compare the post-snap picture with the plan you made before the snap.

Good post-snap question: “Did the safety stay where the shell suggested, rotate into the box, or take away my first answer?” If it changed, use your safe answer.
Madden Practice Mode lab · 4 minutes

Freeze the evidence.

  1. Run the same offensive formation against a two-high defense, then a one-high defense.
  2. Before each snap, say only the shell and your first check: “two-high, count the box” or “one-high, find the extra defender.”
  3. After the snap, pause replay at one second. Where did each safety go?
  4. Write one sentence: “The shell suggested ___; the snap showed ___.”
