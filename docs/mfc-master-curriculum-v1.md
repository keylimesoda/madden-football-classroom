# Football Through Madden — Master Curriculum

## Curriculum map

Madden is the practice field. Football is the logic.

The curriculum starts with the people and formations on the field. It then teaches the run game, the pass game, advanced constraints, and finally how to build a connected offense that can force and answer defensive adjustments.

Only **Chapter 1(a)(i)** has substantive manuscript prose today: offensive personnel. Everything else below is a structured writing target, not a finished lesson.

# Chapter 1 — Basics

## 1(a) Offense

### 1(a)(i) Personnel

Football is like chess. You can win a match just by paying attention to where all the pieces are and how they can move.

It starts with personnel: who do you have on the field?

Offensive personnel uses two numbers. The first number tells you how many running backs are on the field. The second tells you how many tight ends are on the field. The remaining skill players are wide receivers.

| Personnel | Running backs | Tight ends | Wide receivers |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 11 | 1 | 1 | 3 |
| 12 | 1 | 2 | 2 |
| 13 | 1 | 3 | 1 |
| 21 | 2 | 1 | 2 |
| 22 | 2 | 2 | 1 |

Personnel sends a message to the defense about the kind of play you might run. The defense sees your people before the snap, before it chooses its answer.

That message is useful precisely because you can lie with it.

Eleven personnel can spread the field. You can line it up with five eligibles detached and make the defense defend the whole width of the field. Thirteen personnel can look like a heavy run group, then release tight ends into a route combination the defense did not expect. You can motion the same people from one formation into another: 13 personnel can begin in singleback and shift toward an I-formation look; an attached tight end can detach; a back can split out wide.

The personnel stays the same. The problem you give the defense changes.

Most modern rosters do not carry a traditional fullback. In Madden formations that call for one, a backup tight end, power back, or another available player may fill that spot. The label matters less than the job: somebody is being asked to block, lead, release, or create a different backfield picture for the defense.

# Chapter 5 — Planning

## 5(a) Offensive Scheme Development

*Outline only; substantial sub-items to develop.*

### Same concept, different looks

Carry a core run or pass concept from multiple formations, personnel groups, receiver distributions, motions, or condensed sets. The defense must handle the same football problem from a changed geometry.

### Different concepts, same look

Keep personnel and formation stable while attacking different spaces. From **Gun 11 · 2×2 · Y attached right · RB offset**, a player might carry an inside run, an edge run, quick game, play action, dropback concept, and advanced constraint.

### Punch, counter-punch, counter-counter

1. **Punch:** a call that forces the defense to adjust.
2. **Defensive adjustment:** the box, fit, leverage, shell, or pressure answer the defense uses.
3. **Counter-punch:** the space or conflict created by that adjustment.
4. **Counter-counter:** the answer when the defense anticipates the counter.

The aim is rock-paper-scissors, not random play calling.

### Audibles: take connected answers to the line

Audibles are not emergency plays. They are the answer brought to the line for a defensive adjustment the scheme expected.

Build small audible families from one formation:

- base run for the normal box;
- run or perimeter answer for a box overload;
- quick pass for an inserted defender or cushion;
- protection/max-protect answer for pressure;
- shot or counter when defenders squat on the quick answer.

Each audible family needs:

- an **audible trigger**: box overload, nickel insertion, cushion, pressure look, or a defender overplaying the counter;
- an **audible answer**: connected run, quick pass, protection, or shot;
- a stated cost: what the check gives away or what new risk it accepts.

### Call-sheet design

Organize calls by relationship, not just formation-menu location:

- core punch;
- adjustment it provokes;
- counter-punch;
- likely answer to the counter;
- counter-counter;
- audible trigger and answer;
- personnel/formation versions;
- protection and timing requirement;
- game situation;
- Madden version and playbook availability.

The player should be able to say, “They are overplaying my run,” and know the next two calls before opening the playbook.
