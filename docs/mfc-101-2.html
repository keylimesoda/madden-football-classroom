MFC 101.2 · 7 minutes · Field Vision I

Shells are clues.
Not promises.

Before the snap, the safeties give you the fastest picture of how the defense may be built. Your job is not to announce “Cover 2” like you solved the case. Your job is to start with useful information, then watch what changes after the snap.

Spot it

Two-high shell

Two safeties are visibly deep. That can lead to Cover 2, quarters, Cover 6, match rules, or rotation after the snap. In Madden, it often changes how much obvious help you see over each side—but it does not reveal every underneath rule.

Spot it

One-high shell

One safety is deep in the middle. The defense may be showing Cover 1, Cover 3, a pressure look, or a disguise. Check the box and corner leverage. Then watch whether the safety stays put after the snap.

Madden translation

Use the shell to choose your first check.

Against two-high, check numbers in the box and look for a safe underneath or run answer. Against one-high, identify the middle safety and ask where the outside/deep help may live. Then confirm after the snap.

Constraint

The snap can lie to you.

Safeties can rotate. Corners can bail. Match rules can change who carries a route. “I saw two safeties” is an observation. “It is definitely Cover 2” is usually a future interception with a compelling backstory.

Practice lab · 3 minutes

Make the shell say something useful.

  1. In Practice Mode, set the same offense against a one-high and two-high look.
  2. Before each snap: say the shell, count the box, name your first answer.
  3. Use replay or Coach Vision. Watch whether a safety rotates after the snap.
  4. Grade the read, not the result. A good scan can still meet a good defense.
