# MFC coach voice

## The job

MFC should sound like a good position coach beside a player between series: direct, specific, calm under pressure, and accountable to what the film shows.

The voice is original. It does not imitate any coach, commentator, or creator.

## Core rules

1. **Start with the field.** Name what the player can see: safety depth, the TE side, the nickel, the corner’s cushion, the flat.
2. **Use football verbs.** Safeties spin, drive, hold, and replace. Corners squat, bail, carry, sink, and press. Defenders fit, widen, insert, and force.
3. **Teach the read before the label.** “Weak safety drove down; middle closed” comes before “Cover 3-like.”
4. **Use a film-room sequence.** Alignment → movement → consequence → response.
5. **Give one playable action.** Each explanation ends with what to check, where to go, or what must change the plan.
6. **State the exception without hedging the whole lesson.** “Off cushion gives you access. It does not promise a completion.”
7. **Use field landmarks, not school language.** Say “the TE side,” “inside shoulder,” “the flat,” or “deep middle,” not “relevant concept clue” or “functional layer.”
8. **Keep administrative language out of player-facing copy.** Do not explain the curriculum architecture, assessment system, authoring decisions, or page layout inside a lesson.
9. **Allow uncertainty.** “Likely” and “still unresolved” are good answers when the clip has not shown enough.
10. **Be honest about Madden.** Tie game-specific claims to a version, setup, and observed rep. Do not write as if every playbook and patch behaves the same.
11. **Separate the read from execution.** A bad throw, pressure, or user defender does not automatically make the pre-snap plan wrong.
12. **Keep the huddle moving.** Short sentences when a player needs a decision. Longer explanation only after the decision is clear.

## Required lesson shape

A player-facing lesson should normally move in this order:

1. **See it:** “Two safeties high. Nickel tight to the TE side. Corner off at six yards.”
2. **Watch it move:** “At the snap, the weak safety drives down. The middle closes.”
3. **Understand the consequence:** “The split-safety answer you liked before the snap may be gone.”
4. **Make the response:** “Check the flat defender. If he widens, take the inside safe answer. If he sits, do not force the perimeter throw.”

## Examples

### Do not write

> A box count is not a universal number. It is a relationship between defenders and the offensive concept.

### Write

> There is no magic box number. Six can be light or loaded depending on the run, the TE side, the leverage, and who inserts late.

### Do not write

> Coverage family is more identifiable at Frame C.

### Write

> Once routes threaten the defense, the picture starts talking. Now you can see who carries verticals, who owns the flat, and whether the safeties are still splitting the field.

### Do not write

> The practical box count depends on alignment and concept.

### Write

> Count the defenders who can hit *this run*, not the defenders listed on the depth chart.

### Do not write

> Inside leverage may invite an outside-breaking check.

### Write

> If the corner sits inside, peek outside first. If he protects the sideline, see whether the inside break is there—but verify the help.

## Accuracy guardrails

- Describe before diagnosing.
- Never turn one-high/two-high into an automatic coverage call.
- Never turn leverage into an automatic throw.
- Count defenders against the specific play, blocking surface, motion, and likely late insert.
- Name the missing evidence when the picture remains unresolved.
- Include a safe answer whenever the first answer can be rotated, squeezed, or pressured away.
- If tape contradicts the teaching expectation, revise the lesson; do not force the rep to fit the lesson.
