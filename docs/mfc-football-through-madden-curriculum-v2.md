# Madden Football Classroom — Football Through Madden

## Curriculum outline v2

**Status:** New source manuscript. Replaces the superseded Field Vision-first outline. Presentation work is frozen until each module has substantive instructional writing and example material.

## Course promise

MFC teaches football through the decisions a Madden player must make. The learner starts with the people and geometry on the field, then learns how runs, protection, routes, pressure, and coverage create conflicts. Madden supplies the practice field; football supplies the logic.

## Guiding order

1. Learn who is on the field and where they line up.
2. Learn how offenses attack the run and how defenses fit it.
3. Learn how offenses protect and build passing concepts.
4. Learn how defenses rush, cover, disguise, and answer those concepts.
5. Learn the matchup: which answers stress which structures, and what the counter looks like.

# Part I — Formations: the picture before the play

## 1. Offensive formations

**Core question:** Who do I have on the field, where are they lined up, and what can that formation become?

### 1.1 Personnel

- Personnel notation: 00, 10, 11, 12, 13, 20, 21, 22.
- How the two numbers identify running backs and tight ends; the remaining skill players are wide receivers.
- Why personnel is a pre-snap message to the defense.
- How offenses use personnel and alignment to deceive: heavy personnel releasing into pass concepts; spread alignments from normal personnel; shifts and motion that change the formation after the defense has declared its call.

### 1.2 The offensive core

- Center, guards, tackles, and the stable five-man line.
- Attached tight end, strong side, weak side, and what an extra edge blocker changes.
- QB location: under center, pistol, shotgun.
- Backfield structure: halfback depth, offset back, fullback, H-back, lead blocker, split flow.
- Madden roster reality: traditional fullback roles are often filled by a backup tight end or another available player.

### 1.3 Receiver distribution and formation families

- 2×2, 3×1/trips, bunch, stack, condensed splits, empty.
- How distribution changes defensive communication, coverage spacing, run strength, motion rules, and route-combination options.
- Canonical tutorial baseline: shotgun 11 personnel, 2×2 — X and slot left; attached TE and Z right; RB offset beside QB.

**Required diagrams:** personnel family comparison; shotgun 2×2 vs. I formation; 2×2 vs. 3×1; bunch and stack.

**Ric draft:** This module starts from Ric’s long-form offensive-formation draft. It is the first manuscript to develop fully.

## 2. Defensive formations

**Core question:** What front, box, and secondary structure is the defense using to answer the offense’s people and formation?

### To be drafted by Ric

- Even and odd families as visual organization.
- Base, nickel, dime, and specialty personnel.
- Front/box/perimeter/deep layers.
- How defensive alignment shifts to formation strength, motion, bunch, trips, and empty.

**Canonical starting diagram:** shotgun 11 personnel 2×2 against standard 4-2-5 nickel. Odd/tite, over/under, and other families arrive as controlled variations.

# Part II — The run game

## 3. Offensive rushing

**Core question:** What is this run trying to do, where is it designed to hit, and which defender must be displaced, reached, kicked out, or made wrong?

### To be drafted by Ric

Madden naming and football principles:

- blast;
- dive;
- power;
- inside zone;
- outside zone;
- pitch/toss.

For each run family, the eventual lesson needs:

- Madden name(s) and playbook labels, marked by game version;
- aiming point and intended run lane;
- line/blocking story;
- role of TE, fullback/H-back, puller, or motion player;
- first unblocked defender / read player;
- common defensive answer;
- Madden Practice Mode rep and replay question.

## 4. Defensive rushing

**Core question:** How does the defense account for every gap, set the edge, and keep the ball from escaping outside?

### To be drafted by Ric

- Even, odd, over, and under fronts.
- Gaps, fits, spill/box/force language where appropriate.
- Run fits from the front through the second level.
- Contain and edge responsibility.
- Pre-snap run-picture review: front, box count, safety depth, overhang/nickel position, force player.

**Sidebar:** Untarget defender — explain the defender the offense intentionally leaves for the runner/read to make wrong, and distinguish that from a blown blocking assignment.

# Part III — The pass game

## 5. Offensive passing: protection and pre-snap answers

**Core question:** Before routes matter, can the quarterback identify the pressure picture, set protection, and understand the price of each adjustment?

### Requires joint development

Pre-snap protection options and risk/reward to enumerate and verify in Madden 27:

- base / slide protection;
- half-slide and full-slide concepts where exposed by the game;
- ID Mike and what it actually changes;
- keeping the RB in to block;
- releasing the RB late / check-and-release;
- keeping TE or H-back in;
- max protect;
- motioning a player to change the edge or protection count;
- quick-game answer versus pressure;
- hot route / sight-adjustment answer;
- using a back or TE as a chip player, if the game supports it.

For each: when to use it, what it gives up, what it cannot solve, and what a player should verify after the snap.

## 6. Offensive passing: route families

# Part IV — Defending the pass

## 8. Pass rush and pressure concepts

**Core question:** How does the defense create pressure without simply sending bodies, and how does the quarterback recognize the risk before the snap?

- Four-man rush and simulated pressure basics.
- Stunts/twists.
- Mug looks and walk-up threats.
- Zone blitz.
- Creepers/simulated pressure where the game behavior supports teaching it.
- Blitz path, protection count, escape lanes, and why pressure changes route timing.

**Sidebar:** In man coverage, a defender whose assigned player stays in to block or does not release can become an extra blitzer. Explain the football rule/behavior carefully and verify Madden-specific behavior before publishing.

**Sidebar:** QB contain — why rushers keep the quarterback in the pocket, what contain sacrifices, and when the offense can punish it.

## 9. Coverage structure and coverage types

**Core question:** How does the defense divide receivers and grass after the snap?

### 9.1 Coverage families

- man;
- zone;
- match.

Define the difference through defender behavior, not just play-call names.

### 9.2 Zone and match responsibility vocabulary

Develop clear explanations and diagrams for:

- flat;
- squat flat;
- cloud flat;
- curl-flat;
- hook curl;
- seam/hook variants as used by the game;
- deep third;
- deep half;
- deep quarter;\n- match/quarters distribution where applicable.

### 9.3 Shells and coverage families

- Cover 0 / pressure-man context;
- Cover 1;
- Cover 2;
- Cover 3;
- Cover 4 / quarters;
- Cover 6;
- Cover 9;
- split-field and rotating presentations.

Teach safety tracking as pre-snap presentation → post-snap rotation → route distribution. Do not teach a shell as an automatic coverage call.

**Sidebar:** Pre-snap adjustments — alignment, press/off, shade/leverage, coverage audibles, safety/nickel adjustment options, and the tradeoff each adjustment creates. Madden-specific controls require version-labeled verification.

# Part V — Matchups and answers

## 10. Route concepts versus coverage structures

**Core question:** What does a concept stress, what is the defense trying to take away, and what is the next answer when the first answer is removed?

This should become a large, filterable matrix after the prerequisite modules are written and tested.

Matrix dimensions:

- offensive formation/distribution;
- route concept;
- protection / time requirement;
- primary defender conflict;
- likely coverage-family response;
- man, zone, and match variation;
- pre-snap clues;
- post-snap confirmation;
- quarterback progression;
- defensive counter;
- offensive counter;
- Madden-specific caveat/version note.

The matrix must not become a “beat this coverage” cheat sheet. Every cell needs the counter: what the defense can do to take away the apparent answer.

# Part VI — Advanced offense

## 11. Constraint football: make the defense wrong twice

**Core question:** Once the defense has learned to answer the base run and pass game, how does the offense use conflict, timing, and formation movement to punish that answer?

This section follows the core run and pass curriculum. It assumes the learner already understands personnel, fronts, run fits, protection, route concepts, and coverage structure.

### 11.1 RPOs

- The run/pass conflict defender.
- Pre-snap box and leverage clues versus the actual post-snap read.
- Why an RPO is not simply a handoff with a screen attached.
- Run, throw, and abort decisions; where Madden's implementation differs by play, version, or animation.

### 11.2 Option football

- Read option, speed option, triple-option concepts where Madden supports them.
- Dive, quarterback, and pitch responsibilities.
- Force/contain defender and the defender deliberately left unblocked for the read.
- When the defense scrapes, exchanges, spills, or keeps the quarterback contained.

### 11.3 Play action

- How a credible run picture changes second-level and safety behavior.
- Under-center versus shotgun play action.
- Protection and route timing: play action can buy space, but it can also delay the quarterback and expose pressure.
- How to distinguish useful play action from calling a long-developing pass into an obvious pressure look.

### 11.4 Motion and shifts

- Shift: changing the formation before the snap.
- Motion: moving a player through or across the formation before the snap.
- What movement reveals about man/zone indicators, strength, force players, coverage checks, and run-fit adjustments.
- How motion changes formation strength, surface count, protection, and route release.
- Madden-specific motion controls and limitations, version verified before publishing.

**Production rule:** Advanced offense is not a bag of tricks. Every lesson must identify the base concept being constrained, the defender placed in conflict, the defensive answer, and the counter to that counter.
