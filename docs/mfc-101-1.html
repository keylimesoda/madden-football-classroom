The Pre-Snap Scan

Before the snap, you do not need to predict every coverage. You need a disciplined starting point: identify the picture, choose a first check, and know what evidence will change your mind.

Decision skill: make a pre-snap plan without forcing a pre-snap conclusion.
In this course: ObjectiveWorked readFour questionsMadden labCheck
Learning objective

Turn the picture into a plan.

By the snap, say: “I see a two-high shell, seven defenders near the line, and an off corner to the trips side. I will check the run count, then confirm the safeties after the snap.”

That statement is useful because it separates what you can see now from what must be confirmed after the snap.

The repeatable method

Four questions, always in this order.

01 · SHELL

Where are the deep safeties?

Start with one-high or two-high. It locates the defense’s visible deep help. It does not name Cover 1, Cover 2, Cover 3, or Cover 4 by itself.

02 · BOX

How many can affect the run now?

Count players near the line, then compare them with your blockers and runner. You are looking for numbers, not a guarantee.

03 · LEVERAGE

What is the nearest defender taking away?

Off coverage gives cushion. Press changes the release. Inside or outside leverage hints at the defender’s first priority.

04 · PLAN

What is your first check and safe answer?

Name a first answer and an escape hatch: run if the count stays favorable; work the quick outlet if they rotate; do not force either if the post-snap picture disagrees.

Madden Practice Mode lab · 3 minutes

Say the scan out loud.

  1. Choose one offensive formation with a run and a quick pass available.
  2. Set the defense to a two-high look, then a one-high look.
  3. Before every snap, state: shell, box, leverage, plan.
  4. Run the play. In replay, identify the first thing that changed after the snap.
  5. Repeat until the scan takes fewer than five seconds and still produces a clear plan.
